How the model works

  • Team ratings. Each team starts from its preseason points over/under, and its play this season gradually takes over: a third of the rating after 20 games, half after 40, two-thirds by season’s end. This season’s play is measured mostly by expected-goal differential (70%) and partly by actual goal differential (30%).
  • Games. Each remaining game is played out by drawing each team’s goals from its rating, with a small home-ice edge. Ties go to overtime or a shootout, and the loser gets a point. The share of games reaching overtime is matched to the NHL’s actual rate.
  • Uncertainty. Each simulated season draws its own team strengths around the ratings, so the forecast allows for teams being better or worse than they’ve looked so far.
  • Playoffs. The top three teams in each division and two wild cards per conference qualify, with ties broken by regulation wins. The bracket is played out in best-of-seven series, with home ice going to the team with more points.
  • Draft lottery. The 16 non-playoff teams go into the NHL’s two-draw lottery, using the official odds. A team can move up at most 10 spots.
